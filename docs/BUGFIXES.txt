DBZ SUPERSONIC WARRIORS 2 PORT - BUGS FIXED
===========================================

PART 35

AC. All-Star: life bars looked empty after a heart, a revive or a new enemy
   From the third enemy on (after Krillin) the enemy's life bar looked empty although its health still counted, a
   fighter brought back with a heart came back with an empty-looking bar, and a fighter healed while waiting on the
   bench showed the healed part as empty. The game keeps a separate set of bar colours for every team member and only
   repaints the colours of the fighter who is on screen when its health changes; health gained while a fighter is
   off screen (hearts, the All-Star mode reusing a defeated enemy's slot for the next enemy) was never painted. In
   All-Star the port now repaints a fighter's bar from its real health whenever it comes in, after every heart and
   when the next enemy is loaded. Checked with captures: every incoming enemy and every healed / revived team member
   shows a full bar in its own colour (the two small bars under the big one too).

AD. All-Star: records screen and space sky redesigned
   The records screen now looks like the game's own results screen (portraits, score table, WINNER banner, ranking
   rows, all in the game's own lettering and digits). The space sky has a gradient, a faint Milky Way and coloured,
   twinkling stars.

PART 32

AA. LAN: the guest saw the host's touch screen while the battle was paused
   When either player paused a LAN battle, the joining player's lower screen switched to the host's team with the
   red Touch! button, and the PING disappeared. The game's pause menu is on the top screen and leaves the bottom
   screen showing player 1's panel; the port drew the guest's own panel in every battle state except the pause. It
   now does, and the PING stays visible; the guest's panel also stays while a battle is being left after the other
   player quit. (Pausing was measured not to slow the game or raise the ping in our tests.)

AB. Faster on slow PCs / Steam Deck (always on)
   The emulated machine now does less bookkeeping per frame (display register capture only when the picture needs
   it, a short path for the 3D command writes, a lighter scanline loop, faster texture-memory copies, reused 3D
   materials): about 10-20 % less CPU per battle frame, with exactly the same output. The new PERFORMANCE option
   (settings page 4) goes further for slow machines.

PART 31

Y. LAN: menus and sprites invisible after the LAN pages
   After opening the LAN multiplayer HOST / JOIN pages and going back, the LOCAL / LAN screen and Mode Select showed
   only their hexagon background, with no menu options or sprites. The port hides the game's other lower-screen
   layers while its LAN pages are open, but nothing turned them back on when the pages were closed with BACK / B
   and no session was running. All the port's pages now share one rule that undoes the hiding whenever no page is
   open, on every exit path, and it is checked every frame as a safety net.

Z. LAN: rejoining a room could start a broken battle and soft-lock the host
   After a player left a room and joined again, information from the previous session was reused: the rejoined
   player could skip the character select, pick a stage on their own and load into a broken battle while the host
   waited in the stage select forever. Every message now carries a session and round id, old information is
   thrown away when a session starts or ends, and a battle only starts on the host's stage result for the current
   round. Battles are numbered by the host (an interrupted battle could freeze every later battle of the session),
   both games tell each other where they are once a second ("OUT OF STEP" / "BATTLE STOPPED" brings both back to
   the room if they drift apart), and joining a host still on its way back to its room shows "HOST NOT IN ITS ROOM -
   TRY AGAIN". Leaving, closing the window or a crash of either player at every step was tested to end with both
   players back in their room / the LAN page with a notice.

PART 29

W. SAME CHARACTERS: supports could start the battle
   With SAME CHARACTERS a team could take two or three supports, or only supports; a support could then be the
   first fighter, and two supports could never be called from the touch screen. The game's own rules now apply
   again to supports: a team takes at most one support and needs at least one fighter, who starts the battle.
   Fighters can still be repeated (three Gokus).

X. Local multiplayer: PLAYER 2's Touch! stayed dark / supports one at a time
   PLAYER 2's Touch! button stayed dark at 200 % at the start of a battle even when the team had a Team Arts: the
   game only works out the Team Arts partner for player 1 when the battle loads. It is now checked for both players,
   so the button lights exactly when a tap would start Team Arts. And only one support could be on screen for both
   players together (the game has a single support slot); in local play each player now has their own, so both
   supports can fight at the same time, even the same character.

PART 28

U. Garbled sky for one frame when STAGE SHUFFLE changes the stage
   At each stage change the frame right after it showed a garbled background (purple checkers or rays) over the
   old stage. The game's stage loader switches the screen mode at once but sends the new background graphics only
   at the next screen refresh. The mode change now waits for the graphics, so the picture goes straight from the
   old stage to the new one.

V. Local multiplayer fixes
   - Character select: after one player picked a full team of high-cost fighters, every face went black for both
     players. The game keeps the D.P of the current side's slots in a few bytes that were left on the full player's
     team; each player is now checked against their own team.
   - Shuffle cheats: when both players changed character in the same moment, a top-screen face icon showed the
     other player's face or the right face in the wrong colours (e.g. Frieza's colours on Cell). Each player now
     has their own buffer for the new icon.
   - Two supports (SAME CHARACTERS) overlapped on the touch screen and could not be called; the support box is now
     split in half, one support per half, and each half calls its own support.

PART 26

R. Slow on Steam Deck / Proton
   Under Proton on a Steam Deck the game ran at about 40 fps in battles (dips to 17) and below 60 in menus, at
   any resolution. Each displayed frame ran the game's frame and then Godot's drawing and present one after the
   other, and on a slower CPU the catch-up made it worse. When frames come late, the game now computes the next
   frame on its own thread while the picture is drawn and presented (fast PCs are unchanged; the game itself
   behaves exactly as before). An unknown screen refresh rate (as under Proton) no longer adds a 60 fps limiter
   on top of V-Sync. To measure: DBZ_PERFLOG=1 %command% in the Steam launch options writes perflog.txt.

S. Bomb Rain bombs broke apart while falling (FRAME RATE above 60)
   With smooth motion on, the falling bombs' pixels drifted apart, and for a moment a bomb could turn into a
   grey or dark box or partly vanish. Each bomb was drawn from about 40 small rectangles that all looked the same
   to the smooth-motion system, which then moved them with each other's motion. Each bomb and its shadow is now
   a single textured picture like the game's own sprites, in a small piece of texture memory reserved through
   the game's own texture manager, so it always moves as one piece.

T. Damage numbers, blood, confetti and sparkles glitching with smooth motion
   For the same reason, a damage number could break up for a moment into red and black blocks, blood drops
   stretched into streaks and KO confetti / FLASHY KI sparkles jumped about at FRAME RATE above 60. Each piece
   now carries its own invisible tag, so it only follows its own motion. The 60 fps picture is unchanged.

PART 24

N. Mirror Mode made the pause menu unreadable
   With MIRROR MODE on, pausing a battle (Start) showed the game's pause menu flipped, so its text read backwards.
   The mirror was applied whenever a battle was on screen, including the pause state. The picture (and the
   LEFT/RIGHT swap) is now normal while the battle is paused and flips back when the fight goes on.

O. Two sounds on B in the HI-SCORES question
   Pressing B on the "ARE YOU SURE?" page played the back sound and the decide sound at the same moment. B (and
   NO) now play only the back sound, like UNDO on the window-size question.

P. Long gamepad names drawn over the box edges (CONTROLS)
   Seven-letter gamepad names such as D-RIGHT, R-STICK or PADDLE1 were wider than their boxes and covered the
   orange rims. A name too wide for the bold font is now drawn in the game's narrower regular font.

Q. The PORT tile's cheat line ran out of its box
   With cheats on, the tile's description said "Cheats on: N (pages 3-6 of the menu)", which ran past the right
   edge of the text box and only appeared after the menu had been opened once. It now reads "Cheats on: N (menu
   pages 4-7)." and is there from the start.

PART 22

H. The game went back to the opening logos (Mode Select)
   Pressing a button in the very same moment you moved onto the middle tile (for example Right and X together on
   MAXIMUM), or clicking the tile while pressing X, let the game "select" the hidden middle tile, which in the
   original code restarts from the Bandai/Cavia logos. Presses that come with the move or the click are now kept
   from the game, so the cursor just lands on the tile or the settings open.

I. The game stopped after the PC was asleep
   A safety check stops the game when it seems stuck for 10 seconds; it also counted the time the PC was asleep or
   frozen, so waking the PC in the middle of a game could leave a frozen picture. Only time the game could really
   run is counted now (tested by freezing the game for 12 seconds: it carries on).

J. settings.cfg edited by hand
   A value written in quotes ("true") stopped the loading half way and the remaining settings went back to their
   defaults. Every setting now loads on its own; a value that makes no sense is replaced by its default.

K. TOUCH FUZZY did not work in Practice (and in the battles' KO / time-up sequences)
   It looked for Free Battle's own scene numbers. It now checks that a battle is on screen (the battle's own
   picture routine is running), which is the same in every mode: Practice, Story, Z-Battle, Maximum, Free Battle.

L. BEVEL EMBOSS outlines stayed visible during fades to black / white
   The emboss was added after the screen fade, so a black screen still showed outlines. The fade is now applied
   after the emboss, so a black screen is black and a half fade shows a half-faded emboss.

M. Menu texts
   The Dragon Ball tile now says the cheats are on pages 3-6 (not 3-5), and the CONTROLS page arrows show
   "PAGE 1 / 2" instead of "/ 7".

PART 21

G. Stage pieces vanished too early when zoomed out (ZOOM 70 / 85)
   Some stage objects only appear while the camera is inside a range the game picked for its normal view (Roshi's
   house on the island), repeating props jump to the other side half a stage away, and the long background strips
   (destroyed city buildings, ruins skyline) are drawn only a few pieces around the view. Zoomed out, the edges of
   the bigger view showed these gaps. While drawing (not for the game itself) the ranges are now widened by the
   zoom and the missing copies / pieces are added; at ZOOM 100 nothing changes. Checked on all 15 stages by
   sweeping the camera across each one at 70 and 85.

PART 20

E. Dash lines stayed on the fighter for a second (NO COOLDOWN cheat)
   With NO COOLDOWN on, the speed lines of a quick double-tap dash stayed on your fighter for about a second after
   the dash ended; the R-dash lines and the glow from charging ki (R) did the same. The cheat lets a beam or super
   keep firing when you cut it short with a new attack, but it was also keeping every other effect that normally
   ends with its move. Now only the beams and shots of the attack you actually cut short keep going; dash lines and
   charge glows disappear the moment the move ends, as in the original (checked frame by frame against the
   original game: 17 frames). With the cheat on and no attack cut short the picture is identical to playing
   without it; a cut-short super (Goku's Spirit Bomb) still flies on.

F. Frame rate at high FPS
   Measured: the game's own work per frame did not get slower in the recent parts (5600 battle frames: 57 s now vs
   61-63 s for the part-17 build and the port without its extras, same machine). The extra cost at very high
   frame rates was in drawing the in-between frames: every 3D material and both screens got their "in-between"
   value set separately on every picture, the window drew a full-size black rectangle under the game every picture,
   and the sharp-scaling shader also ran for whole-number window sizes where it does nothing. Now one shared value
   is set once per picture (only when it changes), the black borders come from the window's own background, and
   whole-number window sizes use plain pixel scaling. The pictures are identical; only the work is less.

PART 19

C. No sounds on the settings tile and in the settings / cheat menus
   The Dragon Ball tile on Mode Select is not a tile the game uses, so the port moved the cursor onto it and
   opened the menu itself, without the game's sounds, and the port's menus had no sounds at all. Now the port
   plays the game's own Mode Select sounds through the game's sound function: cursor move (moving onto the tile,
   moving in the menus, page flips), select (opening the menu, pressing a button) and back (B, EXIT, BACK). They
   follow SOUND FX on/off and VOLUME like every other game sound. Checked by watching the sound chip start a new
   effect channel right after each action.

D. CHAOS SHUFFLE broke grabs, throws and summons
   A swap could happen while you were caught in an attack (Kid Buu's ball super made you invisible and free,
   throws ended at once, some grab supers broke) or while your own super summoned helpers (Ginyu's and Cooler's
   teams vanished). The game marks throws and grabs (both fighters get a "held" flag and special victim states)
   and spawns summoned fighters as objects of the super. CHAOS SHUFFLE now waits while you are held, thrown,
   grabbed or hidden, while either fighter is in a throw or super, and while objects of your super / throw /
   summon are still alive, then swaps the moment it is over. Checked against Kid Buu's ball, a throw, Broly's grab
   super, your own throw, and Ginyu's / Cooler's summons, plus a run through ten characters' supers.

PART 18

A. Boss characters flew backwards after a dash (BOSS CHARACTERS cheat)
   When a dash runs without a direction held, the game sets the speed to (dash value x speed scale), computed in
   32 bits. The strongest boss settings (level 3: Broly, SSJ Goku, Cell, ...) make that product too big; it wraps
   around to a huge NEGATIVE speed, so the fighter shot off backwards across the stage. The original game has the
   same overflow (proven in the melonDS emulator by giving player 1 the boss settings); it just never lets a player
   be a boss. Fix: for cheat bosses only, that one speed is computed with the normal character's scale. Bosses keep
   their HP, boss moves, cheaper supers and faster movement; their dash now goes forward (9960 vs 3984 for normal
   Broly) and slows down normally.

B. Rare freeze during fast character changes (found while building CHAOS SHUFFLE)
   While a character is loaded from the ROM, the port could start the screen-refresh routine in the middle of the
   read; that routine waited for the ROM transfer to finish, which could not happen until it returned, so the game
   froze (seen once in a stress test, at the 16th change). During character and stage changes the refresh now
   leaves its uploads for the next frame. Tested with 100+ character changes and 38 stage changes without a freeze.

PART 17

1. Gap at the screen edge while dashing (smooth motion)
   With FRAME RATE above 60 (your setting: MAX) the port draws extra pictures between the DS's frames by moving
   every 3D point part of the way back to where it was a frame earlier. The DS cuts 3D shapes off at the screen
   edge, and those cut-off corners were moved inward too, so during fast camera moves (dash attacks, knock-backs) a
   thin strip at the edge showed the sky / empty space behind the stage. Now a point that lies on the screen's
   edge is never moved away from it; the shape is just stretched to the edge. Checked with frame-exact captures of
   a dash: the edge strip is gone (stray edge pixels 3836 -> 485, the rest are normal in-between pixels), motion is
   still smooth, and the 60 fps picture is unchanged.

2. Boss skull stayed after CHARACTER SHUFFLE (BOSS CHARACTERS + CHARACTER SHUFFLE)
   A boss you picked with L kept its skull marker after the shuffle turned it into another character, even though
   the new character was a normal one. Now a shuffled boss becomes the boss version of the new character when that
   character has one (boss stats and boss-only moves), and otherwise loses the skull. Checked in a battle:
   Broly (boss) -> Majin Buu showed the skull on a normal Buu before; now the marker follows the real boss state.
